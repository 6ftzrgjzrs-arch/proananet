What is bulimia?

Bulimia nervosa is an eating disorder characterized by repeated episodes of binge eating, often followed by behaviors such as vomiting, fasting, or excessive exercise. People with bulimia may experience strong concerns about food, weight, or body image. The disorder can affect people of any body type and is not always visible from the outside. Its causes can involve a combination of biological, psychological, and social factors. Common symptoms include binge eating, feeling unable to control eating, trying to compensate afterward, and frequent concerns about food or body image. Physical warning signs can include swollen cheeks or jaw area, sore throat, tooth damage, stomach problems, dizziness, weakness, dehydration, and changes in menstrual periods. Symptoms can vary from person to person.

How much do you absorb?

Your body still absorbs roughly 50% to 75% (or more) of the calories you eat, even if you vomit immediately Digestion and calorie absorption begin right away in the mouth with saliva and continue through the esophagus and stomach before vomiting can happen. • Studies on bulimia show that people typically retain about 1,100 to 1,200 calories regardless of how large the meal or binge was, meaning vomiting does not reliably purge ingested energy. Foods high in simple sugars, sugary drinks, ice cream, or smooth liquids digest incredibly quickly. Chemical breakdown and absorption of simple sugars begin almost immediately in the mouth through saliva and through the stomach lining, meaning a very high percentage of these calories are retained almost instantly. Heavy solids, meats, breads, and high-fat foods take much longer for the stomach to break down. While these items physically stay in the stomach longer, vomiting still fails to clear all of them. This is because foods mix together during consumption, and dense foods block the complete emptying of the stomach tract. How to take care of yourself if you have bulimia

Make sure to flush your theeth with baking soda after vomiting. This will help to neutralize the acid and protect your teeth from damage. Also, try to avoid brushing your teeth immediately after vomiting, as this can further erode the enamel. Instead, rinse your mouth with water or a fluoride mouthwash and wait 30-60 minutesbefore brushing gently. Also use high fluoride toothpaste and consider using a soft-bristled toothbrush to minimize damage. Stuff like gum and mouth wash can also help to protect your teeth.

Side effects of bulimia

Bulimia can affect many different parts of the body. Repeated vomiting or other compensating behaviors can cause dehydration and electrolyte imbalances, which may lead to dizziness, weakness, headaches, muscle cramps, fainting, and changes in heart rhythm. Other physical effects can include stomach pain, bloating, constipation, acid reflux, nausea, sore throat, swollen salivary glands, and swelling around the cheeks or jaw. Repeated vomiting can also damage tooth enamel, making teeth sensitive or more prone to cavities. Some people may experience dry skin, brittle nails, hair thinning, feeling unusually cold, or changes in their menstrual cycle. Over time, these effects can become more serious and may affect the heart, kidneys, digestive system, and bones. The effects and severity can vary from person to person.
